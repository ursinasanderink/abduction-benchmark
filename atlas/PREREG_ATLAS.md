## 1. Question and positioning

**Question.** When documented episodes of scientific discovery — successes *and* matched failures —
are coded in one scheme across four layers (problem representation, proposal policy, evaluation
policy, and the sequence of moves), (a) can they be coded reliably; (b) do successes and failures
differ in *what* generative operations were used, in *how* candidates were selected and tested, in
the *order/branching* of moves, or in none of these (chance); and (c) does the coding recover the
process content that prior computational reconstructions found?

**Positioning (binding for all public materials).** This is a *consolidation and empirical test* of
existing process models of scientific discovery — not a new taxonomy. Its category content is
inherited from Kulkarni & Simon (KEKADA), Klahr & Dunbar / Schunn & Klahr, Darden and Craver &
Darden, Bechtel & Richardson, Nersessian, Thagard, Chinn & Brewer, Peirce, Josephson & Josephson,
Magnani, Gooding/Tweney, Dunbar; the residual it contributes, not surfaced under the documented
literature searches, is: one coded scheme applied to **matched successes and failures** with
**inter-coder reliability**; the **sequence** of moves coded as data across cases; the
**representation-change decision** recorded with its inputs on historical episodes; and a
literature-derived, source-tagged **Protocol v0** handed to the benchmark's protocol arm. Nearest
methodological precedents, cited in every output: the VPI *Scrutinizing Science* project (Laudan
et al. 1986; Donovan, Laudan & Laudan 1988 — theses tested against multi-episode history including
losing theories, without inter-coder reliability); Thagard's ECHO encodings (evaluation layer,
multi-case); Gorman et al. 1993 (Bell vs Gray) and Bradshaw 1992 (Wrights vs rivals) as matched
success/failure pairs; Bamford 1996 on Neptune/Vulcan; Trickett, Trafton & Schunn 2009 and Dunbar
1997 as reliability-coded contemporary process; Simonton's historiometry; Barseghyan's scientonomy
database; Holmes 2004.

## 2. Registered hypotheses

- **A0 (literature prior — declared input, not a test):** the four layers are a *super-structure*
  over literature-inherited categories (disposition table §4). If pilot coding shows a prior
  category system subsumes ours *better* (fewer uncodable moves, higher pilot α), the manual adopts
  it before the freeze and this is reported.
- **A1 (decomposability / reliability):** episodes can be coded with category-level Krippendorff's α
  ≥ 0.667 on usable categories after one manual revision cycle (pilot) and on the confirmatory sets
  without further revision. *Falsified if α stays below 0.667 on most categories of a layer;
  unusable categories are dropped and reported, never merged post hoc.*
- **A2 (policy vs operation):** across matched sets, success and failure members share
  proposal-layer (L2) operations more than they share evaluation-layer (L3) policies — i.e.
  divergence is concentrated in L1b/L3. *Falsified if L2 divergence ≥ L3 divergence in most sets.*
- **A2-rival (BVSR / chance — pre-registered, literature-forced):** success/failure differences are
  not attributable to any coded policy or operation difference under matched information (Campbell
  1960; Simonton 2010–2012). *"Cannot distinguish policy from chance" is an admitted outcome and
  will be reported as such per set.*
- **A2b (sequence structure):** on process-known cases, successful episodes share a recurring
  ordering/branching structure (trigger → move → decision → next) that failed or truncated episodes
  lack. *Tested by cross-case sequence alignment against shuffled-order controls; falsified if
  aligned successes show no more structure than shuffles.*
- **A2c (strategy-level dissociation — Hintikka/Paavola dissent, declared):** move-level and
  strategy-level codes may dissociate; if success/failure separates at strategy level but not move
  level, the Atlas reports that the explanatory variable is not the move.
- **KEKADA recovery (manual validation, set 8):** the manual, applied to Krebs from Holmes's
  reconstruction with Kulkarni & Simon sequestered, recovers K3, K4, K5, K8 (scope determination;
  mechanism-search strategies; confidence modification; the pre-surprise method-transport
  strategy) without post-hoc categories, and the operator populates K1, K2, K6, K7 (expectation;
  goal shift; new problem; decision rule) on the Krebs data. *Contamination declared: K1, K2, K6,
  K7 were built into the manual because KEKADA has them (§7).*

## 3. What the literature already contains (declared prior; ≤ ½ page)

Every school converges on trigger → generate → evaluate/test → revise, iterated. Triggers are typed
(novelty vs anomaly — Aliseda; expectation violation needs a prior expectation — Simon/KEKADA;
anomaly types map to repair types — Darden, Craver & Darden). Responses to anomaly form an
eight-type set (Chinn & Brewer 1993/1998). Generation strategies are catalogued (Darden Tables 15-1
and local strategies; Craver & Darden schema instantiation / modular subassembly / chaining;
Bechtel & Richardson decomposition/localization; Nersessian analogical / imagistic / simulative
modeling and generic abstraction; Thagard's abduction kinds and conceptual combination; Magnani's
manipulative templates; Schurz's patterns), mostly from published records, advisory, without
reliability statistics. Test-order and confidence have explicit rules (Peirce economy of research —
cheapness, caution, breadth, incomplexity, refutability-first; Josephson's six confidence factors;
Darden 15-2; Klahr/Schunn experiment-space heuristics; Tweney's confirm-early/disconfirm-late).
Order and decision rules exist (KEKADA surprise → scope → mechanism search; Darden 15-3 with
escalation; Craver–Darden anomaly-type → redesign-type; Bechtel–Richardson choice points; Chinn &
Brewer explicitly *no fixed order*). The creative/representation-change step is recorded as open by
each school that addresses it (Simon 1992; Schunn & Klahr 1995; Schurz 2021; Sood et al. 2025).
Whether following an explicit procedure helps is mixed in adjacent fields (Mamede deliberate
reflection positive with null replications; TRIZ vs random-word positive; ACH and cognitive forcing
negative). The Atlas tests and consolidates the above; it does not rediscover it.

## 5. Representation audit of the coding scheme (unknown-unknowns pass, done before v0 froze)

Per-state observability: an episode passes through {pre-anomaly · anomaly noticed · candidate held ·
testing · falsified · abandoned · accepted}. Blind spots found and the field added for each:
(i) *considered-but-unwritten* alternatives are unobservable → "unknown" is a counted value, and
the count of unknowns per field is itself reported; (ii) *negative space* — anomalies available but
not taken up → per-episode **ignored-anomalies list** drawn from the secondary literature, with
source tier; (iii) *counterfactual evidence* — what the failure member could have known → the
matching dimension (§6) records evidence available to each member at the declared date;
(iv) *tempo* — rate of moves (Darwin's twenty years vs Wallace's essay) → **elapsed-time** field
per move; (v) *social* — interlocutors per move → distributed tag with names; (vi) *resource* —
cost of a test where known → **cost** field (Peirce). Feature classes not added, with reason:
affective/biographical variables (out of construct); institutional reception (coded only in set 5
as the declared dimension).

## 7. Procedure

**Unit of coding:** the *move* — a dated, sourced episode of ≤ 1 paragraph, with id, date, source,
tier. **Source tiers:** T1 contemporaneous (notebook, letter, dated manuscript); T2
near-contemporaneous (publication ≤ 2 years; testimony ≤ 5 years); T3 retrospective. Sequence-layer
fields are coded only from T1/T2 unless flagged; retrospective-only evidence is flagged on every
datum.

**Coder-independence sequence (binding, every set):** (a) the dossier (timeline of moves with
sources) may be assembled by a research assistant/agent, which may flag gaps and ambiguities but
must not pre-populate categories or suggest interpretations; (b) the operator codes from the
dossier alone into a locked file (committed, hashed) before anyone else codes; (c) the independent
human second coder codes from the dossier alone; (d) the AI diagnostic coder runs only after the
operator file is locked, from the raw dossier, without seeing any coder's output; (e) agreement is
computed without any coder's initial pass having been influenced by another's. For set 9 the
operator is also the historical actor: declared; the AI diagnostic coder on the raw trace is the
independence check there.

**Manual discipline:** pilots 1–3 develop and revise the manual → **MANUAL_v1_FROZEN** after set 8's
recovery check → confirmatory sets coded as-is; every category change documented; **drop, never
merge** post hoc (post-hoc merges may appear only as labeled exploratory analyses requiring fresh
recoding on a separately registered validation sample).

**KEKADA validation (set 8):** dossier from Holmes 1980/1991 and Krebs's own accounts only; Kulkarni
& Simon 1988, Kulkarni 1990, Simon 1992/1995, Klahr & Simon 1999 sequestered from dossier-builder
and operator until coding is locked; then map. Pass rule as in §2. Contamination caveat: K1, K2, K6,
K7 fields exist in the manual because of KEKADA; the test on them is operator-recoverability, not
manual validity. Grasshoff & May 1995 disagreements logged as ambiguity, not resolved silently.

**Reliability:** hierarchical multi-label coding → **Krippendorff's α per category** (nominal; ordinal
for degree-of-change), bootstrap 95% CIs (1,000 resamples over moves within set, unit = move; the
pseudoreplication caveat is stated), minimum **10 double-coded positive instances** per category
(else reported "under-sampled", not "reliable"). Tiers: α ≥ 0.80 → eligible for central
confirmatory claims; 0.667 ≤ α < 0.80 → qualified/exploratory only; < 0.667 → excluded from
substantive inference, still reported. Pilot and confirmatory α reported separately. Second coder:
stratified sample of ≥ 30% of moves, stratified by set × layer, minimum every set represented.
Implementation `atlas/analysis/reliability.py` with unit tests against published worked examples
(verified independently before pilot 2).

## 8. Analysis plan (pre-specified)

**A2 (policy vs operation).** Unit of analysis = the contrast set (n = 7 usable pairs; set 8 and the
process-trace set 4 excluded from A2). Per set and per layer, compute the **Jaccard overlap** of the
success and failure members' coded category sets (L2 categories; L3 categories; L1b response
types). Prediction: overlap(L2) > overlap(L3) within a set. Statistic: exact **sign test** across
sets on the within-set difference; also the paired difference distribution reported. Structured
qualitative comparative analysis per set: for each divergence, classify as *policy* (different
decision under matched information), *operation* (different generative move), or *unresolved*
(no coded difference / cannot tell) — the last is the A2-rival's column. **Wording constraint:**
conclusions of the form "across the selected contrast sets, evaluation-policy differences were
more consistently observed than proposal-operation differences", never a general law from n ≤ 9.

**A2b (sequence).** Process-known cases only (sets 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9; set 1 if T1 sources suffice).
Each episode → ordered string of tokens (layer-category ⊕ decision-rule tag). Pairwise alignment
by Needleman–Wunsch with identity substitution (match 1 / mismatch −1 / gap −1) and by longest
common subsequence; report normalized alignment scores. **Control:** 1,000 within-episode
shuffles per pair; report the exact permutation p per pair and the distribution across pairs.
Motif extraction: frequent subsequences of length 2–4 across successes vs failures (descriptive).
No inferential claim beyond per-pair permutation p and descriptive motif tables.

**A2c.** Strategy-level tag per episode compared across success/failure alongside the move-level
result; report agreement/dissociation.

**A1 gate.** If α < 0.667 on most categories of a layer after the frozen manual: that layer's
categories are excluded from confirmatory claims; if on most layers: the honest negative result is
published ("documented discovery processes resist reliable coding at this grain") and the
program's Phase-2 layer-labeled tasks are demoted per the program's kill criterion.

## 9. Outputs and boundary

Public: this registration; the manual (v0 → v1 frozen, diffs); the coded corpus for sets 1–6 and 8
(YAML per case, sources cited); the reliability and A2/A2b analyses with code; Paper A
("The Abduction Atlas: Matched Successes and Failures in Documented Scientific Discovery" —
consolidation + empirical test framing); case articles. Private: sets 7 and 9 (content, methods,
results); public reporting of them is abstracted and domain-generic only. The **Protocol v0** handed
to the benchmark's C2/C5 arm is derived from §3 + MANUAL_v1_FROZEN and freezes with the
benchmark's task-materials registration; every step is source-tagged.

## 10. Kill criteria and honesty rules

A1 failure → negative result published (§8). Pseudoreplication guard: moves within a case are not
independent; unit = set. No historic case is used for LLM *testing* here (that is the benchmark's
L3 tier, with transform + canary). Every load-bearing historical claim in any output carries a
source and tier. Categorical negatives about the literature are written as "not surfaced under
the documented queries" with the query log attached.
